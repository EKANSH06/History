Caesar's Killers Had Very Poor Event Planning

Sources confirm the Senate meeting had a strict "No Weapons" policy that senators apparently didn't read
— ⚔ ⚔ ⚔ —

ROME, 44 BCE — In what historians have called "the most disorganised coup in recorded history," sixty senators stabbed Julius Caesar twenty-three times on the Ides of March, despite the fact that literally everyone had been warned. The fortune-teller Spurinna had explicitly cautioned Caesar to "beware the Ides of March," which most HR departments would consider adequate notice.


Caesar, allegedly dismissive of warnings, reportedly waved off his wife Calpurnia's concerns about the whole "dying today" situation. When Brutus — reportedly his close friend — joined the stabbing, Caesar is famously said to have uttered "Et tu, Brute?" — Latin for "Not you too, mate?"


Of the twenty-three stab wounds, only one was fatal, leading historians to speculate that Roman senators needed significantly better aim or perhaps a group therapy session to improve coordination. Caesar's ghost was reportedly spotted walking around looking confused for several years afterward.

Pharaoh Ramesses II Refuses to Age: "It's Called Mummification, Look It Up"
🐪

The longest-reigning pharaoh of Egypt, Ramesses II, reportedly ruled for 66 years and fathered approximately 100 children. Medical experts examining his mummy found evidence of arthritis, bad circulation, and what one historian described as "an absolutely heroic amount of hairgel made from animal fat."

🏺 Arts & Culture
Review: The Colosseum Opens — "Loud, Bloody, Sold Out"
🗡️

The new entertainment complex opened to rave reviews from the Roman public this year (80 AD), boasting 50,000 seats, a retractable awning for shade, and absolutely zero refund policy. Lions rated 4/5 stars. Gladiators described the experience as "very much not ideal." Emperor Titus reportedly threw in free food and wine, raising questions about work-life balance.
